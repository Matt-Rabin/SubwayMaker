SUBWAY BUILDER

Design a rapid transit network. Move millions of commuters. Don't go broke.

How to play

The Population and Jobs overlays show where real people live and work. Every weekday the city's real commuters choose between driving, walking and your subway based on total travel time, fare, parking and congestion. Build a network that beats the car.

  • Station (2) — the first click sets the centre, then move the mouse to turn the platform and click again to place it. Right-click cancels. Click existing track to drop a station straight onto it. Click a station to add a transfer platform on another level (↑/↓ picks the level, move to turn it, click to place). Select a station and click its name to rename it, or change its construction, platform length (which limits train length) or turn it.
  • Transfers — platforms of one station form a transfer complex: changing costs 1.5 min plus 1 min per level climbed. Stations within walking distance can also be linked as a transfer from the station panel.
  • Existing transit overlay — with the Select tool, click a real station or line to add it to your plan. A station on two lines gets a platform on each, lined up with its line; real stations close together become one transfer complex.
  • Plans — everything you build is placed as a blue blueprint with a projected cost. Press Build (or Enter) to pay and construct it, or Discard.
  • Track shape (T) — Straight, Curve (start, bend point, end) or Freeform (bends smoothly out of the last piece). ↑ / ↓ switch elevation: Deep Bore, Cut & Cover, At-Grade, Elevated.
  • Track (3) — lays track only. Click a start (station, track end, open ground, or an existing track to branch off it), then click to add control points; double-click or Enter ends the track, or click a station / track end / track to join it. Corners are rounded automatically into curves of at least 60 m radius, and track leaves a station straight along its platform. Drag control points to reshape any track, Ctrl+click a track to add one, double-click one to delete it. Right-click cancels.
  • Branching: with the Track tool, click any point on existing track to put a junction there and branch off it. With the Station tool, click a track to insert a station into it. Trains can't reverse at a junction.
  • Follow roads (R): while laying track, the line follows the street grid between the points you click, rounding corners into curves.
  • Water & buildings: At-grade can't cross water. Elevated bridges cost 4×, Cut & Cover immersed tubes 6×, Deep Bore 3×. At-grade, Elevated and Cut & Cover pay to demolish every building in their path (more for taller buildings). Deep Bore goes under them.
  • Route (4) — click stations in order along built track. Stations passed on the way are added as stops (Shift+click to run express and skip them). Enter opens the route.
  • Demolish (5) — remove unused stations and track. No refunds.
  • Construction types: At-grade is cheap but demolition in dense areas costs a fortune; Cut & Cover is a shallow tunnel; Elevated can bridge rivers; Deep Bore goes anywhere, under anything, at a price.
  • Add trains and cars to a route to shorten headways and add capacity. Overcrowded lines (red in the Ridership overlay) lose riders.
  • Revenue comes from fares. Trains, stations and track cost money every day. Issue bonds in the Finance tab if you need cash.
  • Camera: drag to pan, scroll to zoom, right-drag (or Ctrl+drag) to rotate and tilt the 3D view. Pause: Space. Speed: [ and ].
  • Demand is real: residents from the 2020 Census and home→work commutes from the Census LODES dataset. The map is OpenStreetMap.
